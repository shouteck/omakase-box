## Why

`create.ts` scans the files that will ship (lines 157–177) and the published patches. It does not scan the title, the one-line why, the task text, or the rendered issue body. Those are the most public strings the tool produces, and the task is exactly where an agent might paste a token, a connection string or a private URL while explaining the work.

The scan is not proof of absence — its limits are stated in BOUNTY.md and in the backlog (binary files, base64, split lines, non-secret names). The gap is that the most exposed text is not read at all.

## Outcome

Title, why, task text and the rendered issue body pass the existing `SECRET_RULES` at `create` and again at `post`. A hit names the field and line, never the value, is **recorded** at `create` (as file hits are today: "a hit holds nothing here and is recorded", `create.ts`), and **holds** the bounty at `post` through the existing `publish-held` wait for the owner, settled by `settleScanFindings`. `--preview` states the scan's limits in one line.

## The change

1. In `create.ts`, scan the three inputs with `scanText` and a field label: `(title)`, `(why)`, `(task text)`. Merge the hits into the plan's existing `scan` result.
2. In `post.ts`, scan the rendered body before the issue is created; a hit is the same hold, not a second mechanism.
3. Print one line in `--preview` and `create`'s summary: the scan reads text for the shapes listed in BOUNTY.md; binary files, base64, split lines and names that do not look like secrets are not covered.
4. Do not add, remove or soften a rule; do not print the matched value.

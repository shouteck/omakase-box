## Why

box bounty is two-sided. A bounty is worth posting only if someone answers it, and worth answering only if it pays and is real.

- **The plan's bet:** bounty sites are short of tasks, not solvers, and a tool in the agent's hand is where tasks come from (PLAN.md, "The bet"). It is unproven.
- **The history is a warning:**
  - Algora's activity on GitHub fell from about 265 issues a month to 1 over the past year (CTO count, 11 October 2026).
  - Bountysource failed.
  - Polar dropped bounties.
  - RentAHuman reports many more workers than tasks, and very few verified payments.

A launch without a strategy for both sides risks the same.

## Outcome

`docs/box-v4/GTM.md`: who the first users are on each side, how they find us, why they stay, what we measure, and the first launch sequence. It is grounded in sourced facts about comparable launches, with experiments that cost nothing, and it ends with one recommended path and the owner's decisions.

## Questions, in order

1. **The market, sized from sources.**
   - How many developers use coding agents today. Which agents and hosts (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Copilot, Devin and others), and how they grew.
   - How many public repositories use agent instruction files (AGENTS.md, CLAUDE.md), measured by GitHub code search if possible.
   - Facts with sources only. Where a number is a vendor's claim, say so.
2. **Who posts first.** Rank candidate first users by pain, reach and willingness:
   - solo developers with agents;
   - open-source maintainers;
   - small startups;
   - agencies;
   - companies with existing bounty budgets.

   For each, record what they would hand off and why. Evidence must come from public discussion or data (issues, forums, posts), quoted briefly with links, not guesses.
3. **Who answers first, and why.** Candidate solvers:
   - bounty hunters from existing sites;
   - developers looking for paid side work;
   - students;
   - people running their own coding agents as solvers (agents answering agents).

   What made solvers trust and return on Algora, Gitcoin, IssueHunt, Opire, Upwork and RentAHuman. **The maintainers' backlash against low-quality AI-generated pull requests:** what is documented, how it would hit us (solutions arriving as AI slop), and what the product already does about it (the poster's agent reads every solution and nothing is run).
4. **Why comparable tools won or failed.** Sources: launch posts, post-mortems, changelogs, public metrics.
   - **Bounty and work platforms:** Bountysource, Polar's bounties, Algora, Gitcoin, IssueHunt, Opire, Replit Bounties, Upwork's early years.
   - **Agent-tool launches that spread:** for example Claude Code, Codex CLI, Cursor, and MCP servers that became popular.
   - What each did for its first 100 users, and which channels worked.
5. **Channels that cost nothing:**
   - **Agent ecosystems:**
     - plugin, skill and MCP registries and marketplaces for the major hosts;
     - npm;
     - the instruction-file block our tool already writes;
     - GitHub (topics, README, a bounty label others can follow).
   - **Launches:**
     - Show HN;
     - Product Hunt;
     - X, with the K41 film;
     - Reddit (the agent communities);
     - Discord communities;
     - dev.to.
   - For each: the audience, what a successful post there looked like (with examples), the rules of the venue (self-promotion limits), and the risks.
6. **Positioning:**
   - One sentence on who it is for and why it beats the alternative, against the honest alternatives: doing the work yourself, asking a colleague, posting on Upwork, Algora or Opire, or opening a plain GitHub issue.
   - Three candidate one-liners, each tested on two fresh agents playing a target user ("would you try this, and why not?"), with their answers recorded.
7. **Revenue, later, without spending now:**
   - Candidate models: a platform fee on rewards, a poster subscription, or free.
   - What comparable platforms charge, with sources.
   - Which model fits the zero-spend start and the trust problem. A recommendation, not a decision: pricing is the owner's.
8. **The cold start, solved in sequence.**
   - **How to seed both sides at $0.** For example:
     - the kitchen and friendly projects posting real $0 or small bounties;
     - solvers' agents invited through the same registries;
     - a public board that shows every bounty even before payment exists.
   - **Which side to start with, and why.**
   - **What would make us stop or change course.**
   - Order and dependencies only, never calendar dates (owner rule).
9. **What we measure.** The plan's free measure (of bounties posted, how many got a solution, and how long it took), plus at most five more:
   - each with how the tool or the platform collects it, without surveillance beyond what the product already records;
   - each with the threshold that would mean the bet is working, or not.

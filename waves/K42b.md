**Brief.** Written by the CTO on 21 October 2026. The owner is based in **Singapore** (owner, 21 October 2026). This wave prepares decision D1 in `docs/box-v4/PLATFORM.md` section 9. **Research only:**

- no accounts or sign-ups;

K42 assumed a Stripe Connect platform that can pay solvers across borders. Stripe's cross-border payouts documentation lists platform locations as the US, UK, EEA, Canada and Switzerland only, and points other platforms to sales. So a Singapore platform on self-serve Stripe Connect could pay Singapore solvers only. This wave finds the best legal, affordable way for a Singapore owner to pay solvers in many countries, and ends with one recommendation for D1. Cook: high; reviewer: high.

## Questions, in order

Every answer comes from a primary source: the provider's own documentation, terms or pricing; a regulator's page (MAS, ACRA, IRAS, IRS); or the statute. Quote briefly and cite. Where only a lawyer or tax adviser can answer, say so and stop there.

1. **Stripe from Singapore.**
   - Can a Singapore Stripe account run Connect at all, and with which account types and charge types?
   - Exactly which countries its connected accounts may be in. Read the cross-border payouts page, its country-specific page and the Connect availability page.
   - Whether an individual (not a company) may hold a Connect platform account in Singapore.
   - What "contact sales for alternatives" covers, from Stripe's public pages only.
2. **Airwallex: Payments for Platforms, connected accounts and Payouts.**
   - Can connected accounts be **individuals**, and in which countries? Find the list for individual payees, not the general "150+ countries".
   - Can the platform collect a card payment from an owner and pay a solver from it without holding funds itself (Airwallex's equivalent of a destination charge)?
   - **Funds:** who holds funds, and for how long. Who carries KYC, disputes and chargebacks. Who files tax reporting.
   - **Access:** is there a self-serve sign-up for the platform product, or only sales? Must the platform be a registered business, and does a Singapore sole proprietorship qualify? Any minimum volume?
   - **Fees:** card fees, payout fees by country, FX, monthly fees. Where pricing is not public, say "not public".
   - **Test mode:** is there a sandbox usable before approval?
3. **Other routes for a Singapore platform, on the same points as question 2:**
   - PayPal (Payouts and multiparty, from a Singapore business account);
   - Wise Business batch payments (the owner paying from their own balance, so the platform only instructs);
   - Payoneer;
   - Adyen for Platforms, if it accepts small platforms.

   Drop any route that requires prefunding a platform-held balance, or that has no path for a small, new platform, and say why.
4. **A US company with Stripe** (for example through Stripe Atlas), from primary sources:
   - the one-time cost and the yearly costs (state fees, registered agent, franchise tax);
   - US filing duties for a foreign-owned company (IRS forms, and the penalties for missing them);
   - which solvers it can then pay (Stripe's cross-border list);
   - whether Singapore and the rest of Asia stay excluded.
5. **Stablecoins (K32's design) under Singapore law.**
   - Under the Payment Services Act and MAS guidance on digital payment tokens: is an owner paying solvers USDC from their own wallet, with the tool only preparing the payment, a regulated service for the owner, or for us?
   - What MAS says about stablecoins (the single-currency stablecoin framework) that affects this.
   - Where a lawyer is needed, say so.
6. **Singapore business basics:** the cost and effort of registering a sole proprietorship or a private limited company with ACRA, from ACRA's own pages; and whether either changes the answers above.
7. **Who can be paid, compared.** One table: for each route, the solver countries it can pay (named, or the provider's own list linked), whether individuals can be paid, and the solver's onboarding.

# BT10: a reward, through a site that funds issues (Algora first)

Milestone: BT · Station: packaging (also evidence) · Base: master at the time this cooks, after BT9 is merged · Branch: `wave/BT10` · Timebox: 180 minutes; at expiry, report partial findings and stop

**Pre-brief.** Written by the CTO on 11 October 2026, before BT6's second run. The design is settled with the owner. **This wave spends nothing** (owner, 11 October 2026: "we are just proving the e2e flow works"): no real reward is offered, the kitchen solves its own bounty, and the bounty site is a stand-in. It still needs the owner to name it. The head chef revises this file against the Results of BT6 to BT9 before dispatching.

## Outcome

A bounty can carry a reward. The owner or the agent states the amount; the tool posts it, keeps count of it against the owner's budget, and gives the agent one command to reward a solver. Who is worthy of a reward, and whether anyone is, is the agent's or owner's decision alone. The tool never suggests an amount, never decides who is paid, and never promises anything on the owner's behalf.

## Settled with the owner (11 October 2026)

- **We are a tool on top of a site, not a site.** Algora holds the money, the solvers and the payment. The tool does the steps before and after.
- **The tool does not set or suggest a price or a pricing strategy.** It carries what it is told.
- **Using a solution and rewarding one are two independent commands.** Either, both or neither, in any order, for any solver, as many times as there are solutions. No condition ties them together and there is no built-in waiting period; an owner who wants a delay between using and rewarding writes it in their standing rules.
- **Rewarding means merging the solver's pull request on GitHub, into the bounty branch.** That branch is a throwaway copy, so the merge changes nothing in the owner's real code. It is the signal: the solver's pull request shows as merged, and Algora treats the bounty as earned. Merging a solution locally (`apply`) never touches the pull request, so using a solution can never move money by accident.
- **Whether money then moves by itself is the owner's setting on Algora** (its auto-pay on merge). With it on, rewarding pays. With it off, rewarding marks the bounty as earned and the owner releases the payment in Algora; the tool says payment is waiting for the owner.
- **The only limits are the owner's:** the ceiling for one bounty and the budget, in the standing rules (BT8 reserved the fields). Inside them the agent is not asked.
- **The owner sets the account up themselves, once:** creating it, accepting its terms, connecting it to the repository and adding a payment method. No agent creates an account, accepts terms or handles payment details, in the kitchen or in the product.

## What the CTO read (11 October 2026), and what is still unknown

From Algora's own pages as found by search, and third-party write-ups. Its public documentation would not load on the day (every documentation and pricing address redirected to a missing page), so nothing below is confirmed from the source.

- A bounty is put on a GitHub issue by a comment (`/bounty $<amount>`); a solver claims it in their pull request (`/claim #<issue>`). The Algora app must be installed on the repository.
- Bounties are paid after the pull request is merged; tips (`/tip`) are paid at once.
- Payment after a merge is the maintainer's action by default; auto-pay on merge is an optional setting.
- Fees are paid on top of the bounty by the poster; figures seen ranged from 11% to 19% plus card processing, from pages of different dates.

**Not known, and not needed to cook this wave.** They are needed before the first real paid bounty, which is a later decision of the owner's and not part of BT10. Until they are read, the site-specific piece is built to what the pages above say and is marked in the doc page as unproven against the real site:

1. Does a merge into a branch that is not the default branch count as the bounty being earned? Our pull requests target the bounty branch. If it does not, the reward command needs another route, and a tip is the candidate.
2. The exact commands today for offering, changing and cancelling a bounty, and whether cancelling refunds anything already charged.
3. When the poster is charged: at offer, at merge, or at release.
4. The current fee, any minimum, and the currency.
5. What Algora's terms say about a bounty whose claimed work is merged or used and not paid. The owner needs this as a fact at setup.
6. Whether a program may post these comments under the owner's sign-in within Algora's terms.

## Done when

- `node scripts/verify.mjs test:box-bounty test:box-package` exits 0 on the final commit; the suites reach no network and move no money: they run against a stand-in `gh` and a stand-in for the site. The stand-in for the site is a small local program kept with the tests, and it is also what the real use runs against.

**Offering**

- A reward is stated by whoever creates the bounty, or added later with one command, as an amount and a currency. Stating none means none; nothing in the tool's output proposes one.
- The tool posts the site's comment on the issue under the owner's sign-in and records the amount. The listing states the reward as a fact and adds no promise about who will be paid or when.
- The standing rules decide whether it goes out without asking: an amount inside the ceiling and the remaining budget does; one outside is BT8's waiting state, which the owner answers once and may save as a rule.
- With no rules written, a reward is never offered without the owner's yes.

**Rewarding**

- `reward <id> <n>` merges solution `n`'s pull request into the bounty branch on GitHub and records it. It is independent of `apply`: no ordering, no requirement that the solution was used, more than one solver may be rewarded. If the owner's rules set a delay or a condition, the tool applies that rule and names it; it has none of its own.
- Afterwards the tool reads back what the site shows and reports it as a fact with the time it was read: paid, or earned and waiting for the owner to release. It never reports a payment it has not read back.
- A reward that would exceed the ceiling or the budget is the waiting state, not a refusal the agent must work around.
- The bounty branch is kept until every reward the agent intends has been made; closing the bounty says plainly that rewarding is no longer possible afterwards and how many solutions are unrewarded, as a fact, without advice.

**Counting**

- The record keeps, per bounty: offered, earned, read back as paid, and the fee where the site states it. `summary` and `rules` show the budget: offered and paid against the ceiling and the limit, for the period the owner's rule names.
- The budget is counted from what the tool did and what it read back, and says which is which. If the two disagree, the output says so and uses the larger.

**Withdrawing and changing**

- `withdraw` on a bounty with a reward cancels the offer on the site by the site's own means, reads back the result and records it. If the site reports that a claim is in progress or money is held, that is stated as a fact.
- Changing the amount is one command with the same rules as offering.

**Honest to the owner at setup, as facts, once**

- Bounties and rewards appear under the owner's GitHub name; how the agent treats solvers is visible on their account.
- The site's terms on using claimed work without paying, as read under "What must be read", quoted or summarised with the date read.
- That with auto-pay on and rules that allow it, a solution arriving can end in a payment with nobody asked; BT9's disclosure shows the same sentence.
- What the tool cannot do on this site and the owner must: whatever the reading shows needs a person.

**Everything else**

- **Cases that must fail, each a named refusal that changes nothing:** a reward on a bounty that was not posted to GitHub; an amount that is not a positive number in a currency the site takes; the site's app not installed on the repository (said plainly, with what the owner must do); `reward` for a solution that does not exist or whose pull request is already merged or closed; any reward command when the owner has withdrawn authority.
- **Half-done outside steps recover,** as BT6 built for posting: a comment posted but not recorded, a merge made but not read back. Repeating a command never offers or rewards twice. A test kills the tool between each outside step and the next.
- A stranger's text is never shown as the tool's own; amounts and states read back from the site are parsed to numbers and known words, never echoed as free text.
- No credential, card or account detail is read, stored or printed. The tool works only through the owner's existing sign-in to `gh`.
- The description shown to agents gains at most one line: that a bounty may carry a reward if the owner's rules allow, and where to read them.
- The three hard stops are unchanged and no reward rule can loosen them.

## The real use: the whole flow, with no money

The owner's instruction: no bounty amount, solve it ourselves, prove the flow end to end. So this wave moves no money and involves no bounty site account.

1. **Nothing is posted to the real bounty site.** The Algora app is not installed on the repository for this wave, and nothing is listed on Algora's board: a test bounty there would be seen by real solvers and waste their time. The site's side (accepting an offer, marking a bounty earned, reporting paid) is played by the stand-in the suites already use, run locally.
2. **GitHub is real.** One bounty on `shouteck/omakase-box`, posted from the main checkout with the merged tool. Its reward is offered through the tool's command with the stand-in as the site, so the real issue carries no reward comment and says, as today, that there is no reward. **The owner sees the exact issue text and says "go" before anything is posted.**
3. **The kitchen solves it.** A blind solver, started in an empty folder outside every checkout and given only the issue's address, opens the pull request, using the account signed in on this machine. Say so in the Result.
4. **A collecting session decides,** from the record alone, and exercises both commands for real on GitHub: it uses the solution (the local merge) and it rewards it (the pull request merged into the bounty branch, so it shows as merged). The stand-in then reports the reward as paid, and the tool reads that back.
5. **Also run for real:** one bounty withdrawn with an offer outstanding, and one command interrupted between its outside steps and repeated.
6. Close with the tool's own commands. Anything left outside is listed in the Result by exact name.

**What this proves and what it does not.** It proves every step the tool takes, on real GitHub, and that using and rewarding are independent. It does not prove that Algora itself responds as the stand-in does, that a merge into the bounty branch counts there, or anything about real solvers or real payment. The Result says exactly that. The first real paid bounty is a separate, later decision that needs the owner's approval, a spending limit, the owner's own account, and the facts listed above.

Nothing in this wave is posted, offered, rewarded or withdrawn overnight.

## Review lenses

- [x] **Security:** the reviewer tries to move money, or the count of it, in ways nobody decided. It tries to: get a reward offered or made through `apply`, `status`, the hook or any command that is not about rewards; exceed the ceiling or budget by repeating, racing or interrupting commands; make the tool report "paid" without the site saying so; get a solver's text, a hostile site response or a doctored record to change an amount; get a reward made after authority was withdrawn; get any credential or payment detail into output or the record. It re-runs BT8's attempts to widen the rules, now with money behind them.
- [x] **User experience:** three readers. An owner, played by the reviewer with only the setup output, says what they have authorised, the most that can be spent, what happens with nobody asked, and how to stop it. A fresh agent on a repository whose rules allow rewards posts a bounty with a reward, later uses one solution and rewards another, and says what was paid and how it knows. A stranger, reading only the issue, says what the reward is and what it has and has not been promised.
- [x] **Measurement fairness:** the budget figures. The reviewer re-derives offered, earned and paid by hand from the record and the stand-in site's log for a run with several bounties, a withdrawal, a failed step and a retry, and compares them with what `summary` prints.

## Notes for the worker

- Feature playbook. Model the money first, before any code: the states a reward moves through, which transitions the tool causes, which the site causes, which only the owner can cause, and which cannot be undone. Put that table in the doc page.
- The site-specific part is one small piece behind the same seam BT6 made for GitHub. Do not build a general system for many sites; one real site first.
- The tool has no opinion. Any sentence in output that could be read as advice on whether, whom or how much to pay is a defect; BT3a's banned-words test covers every reward output.
- Reading back is the only source of "paid". If the site cannot be read, the state is "not known", with the time of the last reading.
- Out of scope: a second site; tips as a general feature (only as the fallback route if the reading requires it); splitting one reward between solvers beyond rewarding each separately; invoices, taxes, refunds beyond what the site reports; any change to how solutions are used.
- Everything the wave creates in the temp folder or on the remote is named for the wave or the bounty and removed at close, except what the site itself keeps, which is listed in the Result.

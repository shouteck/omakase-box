# PF13: a solver can connect PayPal, and the owner pays with one click

Milestone: PF (the platform) · Station: packaging · Base: `master at dispatch` · Branch: `wave/PF13` · Timebox: 240 minutes; at expiry, report partial findings and stop
Owner decisions: D-2026-11-01-connect-where-we-can, D-2026-11-01-pay-on-merge, D-2026-11-01-solver-distributor
Horizon: long: start with `/plan` and keep the plan in this file

**Brief.** Written by the CTO on 1 November 2026, from the owner: emulate how Ko-fi connects payment accounts, without using Ko-fi. **Runs after PF11 and before PF12.** Opens with a proof in PayPal's sandbox, and **stops once for the owner** to open a free PayPal business account, which no agent may do.

## Why

PF11 connects Stripe. A solver may have no Stripe, or may not want Stripe's merchant form. The rule the owner set: where a provider lets us confirm the account and see the payment, connect it; where it does not, such as Wise, keep manual details.

## Outcome

A solver connects PayPal by logging in on PayPal's own page. The owner clicks "Pay $45 with PayPal" on the bounty's page and pays on PayPal's page, straight to the solver's account. The board records it from PayPal. The platform holds nothing, takes nothing and pays nothing.

## The proof comes first

In PayPal's sandbox, in a throwaway script outside the repository, in `%TEMP%\kitchen-PF13-*`:

1. **Pay another account:** an order naming the solver's account as [`payee`](https://developer.paypal.com/docs/checkout/standard/customize/pay-another-account/) is paid and captured, and the money lands with the solver, not the platform.
2. **Log in with PayPal:** it returns a stable account ID for the solver, and [`payee` accepts that ID](https://developer.paypal.com/docs/checkout/standard/customize/pay-another-account/). Ask for the fewest details. If it must return an email address, say so: the board's privacy page says it never asks for one, so storing it needs the owner's word; prefer not storing it.
3. **Knowing it was paid:** the capture response or a signed webhook tells the board. Say which holds when the owner closes the page early.
4. **Live, without paying:** read PayPal's pages on the day and report whether this route needs [partner approval](https://developer.paypal.com/docs/multiparty/checkout/immediate-capture/), a registered business or a Singapore-specific limit for live use.
5. **Reach:** from PayPal's own country pages, list which of the 14 countries Stripe refused in K46 can receive. Blogs conflict on this, so do not cite them as fact.

**Stops, each brought to the owner with one recommendation; none is worked around:**

- the route needs partner approval that is not free and open;
- any step needs the owner's money, including a paid registration;
- money passes through the platform's own PayPal balance;
- it is not possible for the board to learn reliably that a payment happened.

If a stop holds, PayPal stays a manual option, and the report says so.

## The build, only if the proof holds

1. **Solver:** "Connect PayPal" beside "Connect Stripe" on `/me/`, and disconnect.
2. **Owner:** "Pay $<amount> with PayPal" on the bounty's page, shown only for the merged author with a connected account. The same payee, limit and duplicate rules as PF11, with the payee only by the account the merged author connected, never from solver text.
3. **The record:** PayPal's confirmation records it, in the states PF11 defined. Refunds and reversals change the record.
4. **Choosing:** where a solver connected both, the owner sees both; Stripe's pay on merge is unchanged. **Pay on merge is not added for PayPal:** saving a payer's PayPal appears to need the approved platform product. The proof says if that is wrong; it stays out of this wave either way.
5. **Manual stays:** Wise and the rest remain under "Can't use these?", as the owner's word and then the solver's. A pasted PayPal.me link is no longer offered once connecting works; PF13 says what happens to links already saved, and removes none silently.
6. **Words:** what a solver is told about PayPal's fees and reversals, in plain terms. No "secure" or "verified" badge: the page says "connected", which is the fact.

## Tests

- Must-fail, each shown failing first: a payee named in solver text; another user's connection; a duplicate and three at once; an amount over the limit; a forged or stale webhook; a login changed hands; a hostile title in the new fields; a key in an error.
- With no PayPal key the pages say "not set up", and manual methods still work.
- The feed and MCP say whether a bounty's author can be paid through PayPal, as a fact, without exposing any account detail.

## Why

Bounties today are GitHub issues scattered across repositories nobody watches. No one, person or agent, can ask "which open bounties in TypeScript could I do?" across all of them. The platform answers that, and gives solvers a reason to trust an owner they have never met, without money passing through us. Our product rules decide much of what belongs:

- facts, never verdicts;
- a stranger's text is never shown as ours;
- the tool never judges a solution;
- private repositories never appear;
- no ratings or rankings of people.

## The first version (owner, 21 October 2026)

1. **The board:** every public bounty across all repositories, with search and filters: language, repository, stated reward, age, number of solutions, and whether an agent or a person posted it.
2. **For solver agents:** a JSON feed and an MCP server (`find_bounties`, `get_bounty`), plus RSS for people.
3. **The bounty page:** the task (marked as the poster's words), how to answer, and its history from the shared memory: posted, solutions, merged, payment recorded.
4. **Owner payment facts:** "this owner: N merged, N recorded paid, N confirmed received by the solver". The solver confirms receipt with one click.
5. **Solver profile:**
   - the ways they accept payment (for example a PayPal.me, Stripe or GitHub Sponsors link, a Wise email, or a wallet address), stored once and shown on their solutions;
   - their **track record as facts**: merged solutions and confirmed payments, with no ratings (owner, 21 October 2026).

**Not in the first version** (design only enough to leave room):

- the owner dashboard with the agent's activity log;
- facts to help sort many solutions;
- public stats;
- search across projects;
- alerts;
- abuse screening.

## The people and their jobs

| Who | Comes to | Must be able to, quickly |
|---|---|---|
| **Solver agent** (first-class) | the feed, the MCP server | Find bounties matching its skills; read the task, repository, stated reward and how this owner pays; know exactly how to answer. Bounded, machine-readable output it can poll cheaply |
| **Solver, browsing** | the board | Find a bounty worth taking: what is wanted, where, the stated reward, how long it has been open, how many solutions are in, and **this owner's payment facts** |
| **Solver, after a merge** | their profile, the bounty page | See they are owed; confirm "received" once paid; keep their accepted payment methods up to date |
| **Owner** | the bounty page, their profile | See their own payment facts as solvers see them; record "paid" (mirroring `config set paid`) |
| **Posting agent** | the CLI | What the platform shows about its bounty, worded for an agent (registered, listed, the solver's accepted payment methods after a merge) |

## Questions to answer, in order

1. **Reference sites, from their public pages:**
   - GitHub's issue search and "good first issue";
   - Algora, Opire and Gitcoin's old bounty pages (archived if needed);
   - Upwork's job list;
   - npm and the MCP registries (how developers and agents browse a catalogue);
   - Linear for restraint.

   For each, list what a list card and a detail page show, what helps decide, and what is noise. Screenshots stay local, not committed.
2. **The machine interface first:**
   - The feed (`/bounties.json` with filters) and the MCP tools `find_bounties` and `get_bounty`. For each: inputs, outputs, field order (what decides first), bounded sizes, paging, caching.
   - The same facts and wording rules as the pages; K25's rule applies: unasked text stays small, and more is given on request.
   - Built from the `box-bounty-format` block, the shared memory events and GitHub facts (K42 section 3.1); nothing the platform cannot know.
3. **What a bounty card shows, and what it must not:**
   - the facts, in the order that decides a solver;
   - **payment is described as what it is:** "the owner pays directly; this owner's record: …". Never "funded", "guaranteed" or "escrow";
   - solver-written text is marked as theirs;
   - no "best solution", no ratings or rankings of people;
   - every number has a source.
4. **Payment facts and track records, designed honestly:**
   - What each count means. "Recorded paid" is the owner's word; "confirmed received" is the solver's.
   - How a solver confirms.
   - What happens when they disagree. Both facts are shown side by side, and nobody's word is presented as the platform's.
   - How the counts resist gaming, such as an owner paying their own second account. Self-dealing is excluded from counts using K42's rule: the payee is not the poster and has no push access.
   - What a new owner or solver with no history sees.
5. **The pages and how they connect:** board, bounty page, solver profile, owner profile, sign-in with GitHub, a short "how it works" for strangers (from K44's positioning if merged).
6. **Every state of a bounty**, as the solver and the owner see it: open, solutions waiting, merged (owed per the owner's rule), payment recorded by the owner, receipt confirmed by the solver, closed without a merge. Also empty states, and errors that name their next step (PL3, carried to the web).
7. **The visual language: minimalism (owner, 21 October 2026: "a fan of minimalism, something like Apple, Muji, Cognition").**
   - **Study** Apple's product pages, Muji's site and product design, and Cognition's site from their public pages. For each, name what makes it minimal:
     - the ratio of whitespace to content;
     - type scale and the number of sizes in use;
     - colour count;
     - how little each page asks of the reader;
     - how motion is used, or not.

     Take the principles, never their identity (no logos, typefaces under licence, product imagery or copied layouts).
   - **The rules that follow:**
     - one typeface family (open licence), at most two weights and four sizes;
     - near-white or warm-paper ground, near-black text and **one** accent;
     - generous whitespace;
     - no shadows, gradients, badges or decorative icons;
     - one primary action per page;
     - every element earns its place or is cut.
   - **Consistency with the launch film** (K41, paper and ink, which leans Muji: natural, unbleached, restraint): same ground, same accent, same type.
   - Never Claude's identity; never anything that could pass for Apple's, Muji's or Cognition's own.
   - WCAG 2.2 AA contrast and keyboard use; responsive down to a phone; light and dark.
8. **Hosting, rechecked:** with no payments, does Vercel's free Hobby plan fit an open-source, free, non-commercial board? Quote its current terms. Compare with K42's Cloudflare recommendation. Recommend one, at $0.

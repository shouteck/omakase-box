## Why

The platform is the first thing of ours a stranger will see that is not a terminal. Two failure modes are common on bounty sites:

- **Too little:** a list of amounts with nothing that tells a solver whether the work is worth taking or whether they will be paid.
- **Too much:** feeds, leaderboards, badges and rankings that say nothing true.

Our product rules already decide much of what belongs: facts never verdicts; a stranger's text is never shown as ours; the tool never judges a solution; private repositories never appear. This wave turns those rules and K42's flows into screens.

## The people and their jobs

| Who | Comes to | Must be able to, quickly |
|---|---|---|
| **Visitor / solver, browsing** | the board | Find a bounty worth taking: what is wanted, in which repository and language, the reward, **its payment readiness** (K42's wording: "payment method on file, within the owner's limit", never "funded"), how long it has been open, how many solutions are already in, and how to answer |
| **Solver, working** | a bounty page, their account | Answer it; set up payouts once (Stripe's own hosted onboarding, not ours); see the status of each answer and payment |
| **Owner** | their dashboard | See what is open and what money is committed, paid and waiting; answer anything waiting for them ("ask me" payments); allow, deny and change limits for repositories, mirroring the CLI's `allow`, `deny` and `config` and nothing more |
| **Owner, checking on the agent** | an activity view | See what their agent did in their name: posted, merged, paid, each with its proof |
| **Solver agent** (first-class) | the feed, the API, an MCP server | Find bounties matching its skills; read the task, repository, reward and payment readiness; know exactly how to answer and when payment happens. All in bounded, machine-readable output it can poll cheaply |
| **Posting agent** | the CLI (and the same API) | Covered by K42 for actions; here: what the platform tells it back (registered, payment state, waiting for the owner), worded for an agent |
| **Owner, managing agents** | a tokens page | Create and revoke an agent's token and see its limits |

## Questions to answer, in order

1. **Reference sites, studied from their public pages:**
   - **Bounty and work sites:** Algora, Gitcoin, IssueHunt, Opire, Upwork's job list, GitHub's issue list and "good first issue" pages.
   - **Interfaces developers trust:** Linear, Vercel's dashboard, Stripe's hosted Express dashboard.

   For each, list what a list card and a detail page show, what helps a solver decide, and what is noise. Use screenshots, kept locally and not committed if they are someone else's design.
2. **The machine interface first:**
   - A feed (JSON, for example `/bounties.json` with filters by language, reward and state) and the MCP tools a solver agent needs: `find_bounties`, `get_bounty` and `payment_status`, at the least.
   - For each: inputs, outputs, field order (what decides first), bounded sizes, paging and caching.
   - The same facts and wording rules as the pages, applying the K25 rule: unasked text stays small; more is given on request.
   - Built from K42's data model and the `box-bounty-format` block; nothing the platform cannot know.
3. **What a bounty card shows, and what it must not:**
   - The facts, in order of what decides a solver.
   - Rules:
     - **Payment readiness** is a fact the platform knows (K42), never a promise or "funded".
     - Solver-written text is marked as theirs.
     - No "best solution", no ranking of people, no star ratings.
     - Every number has a source.
4. **The pages and how they connect:**
   - Board (with filters a solver actually uses), bounty page, sign in, solver account, owner dashboard, activity, agent tokens.
   - A short "how it works" page for strangers.
5. **Every state of a bounty**, as the solver and the owner see it: drafted (not shown), open with no payment method, open with a payment method on file, solutions waiting, merged and paying, paid, closed without a merge (nothing was charged), waiting for the owner. Also empty states, and errors that name their next step (the CLI's PL3 rule, carried to the web).
6. **The visual language:** match the launch film's direction (K41, paper and ink) so the brand is one thing:
   - warm ground, restraint, serif headings, one accent of our own, and our own identity, never Claude's;
   - accessible (WCAG 2.2 AA contrast and keyboard use), responsive down to a phone, light and dark.

### 1. What is good and should not change

The front page has a real point of view: one large line, the product itself as the picture, four tiles that tell the journey, "Some things it never does". The tokens are right for the taste asked for: warm near-white paper, one ink, one blue, one typeface in three weights. The words are plain. This is the standard the rest of the site should meet, not something to replace.

### 2. Why it reads as unpolished

1. **It is two sites.** The front page is centred, large and generous. Every other page is a narrow column pressed to the left with the right half of the screen empty, small grey labels, and rows like a settings screen. Moving from the front page to the board feels like leaving the product.
2. **It has no face.** The top-left says "Open bounty board" in plain text. There is no mark. The tool is `box bounty`, the package `omakase-box`, the site "Open bounty board", "run by omakase-box": three names for one thing. Every reference site has one name and one mark, top left.
3. **The first thing a visitor does next is a dead end.** "Browse bounties" leads to a grey box: "No open bounties on the board yet." At launch the board will be empty, by the owner's own decisions (no bounties of ours, none at $0). The numbers page is a column of zeros and "none yet". These are the two most likely second clicks and neither was designed for being empty.
4. **The navigation has no action.** Five small grey links of equal weight. Every reference puts one filled button at the right end. On a phone ours drops "For agents" altogether.
5. **On a phone the hero's picture first appears as a tall empty black box,** because the terminal draws its story after loading. For a moment the page looks broken.
6. **Sections can be blank until they animate in.** One capture of How it works, mid-scroll, showed most of a screen empty. "Sections rise as they arrive" should never mean content that is absent until a script runs; it also matters for PF39 and for any reader without scripts.
7. **The install line is one click away from where a developer expects it.** The convention for developer tools is the command itself in the hero, with a copy button (Evil Martians' study; Resend, Vercel and others). Ours is behind "Get started".
8. **The reading pages are dense.** For agents is a label column beside paragraphs of five and six lines mixing prose and commands. How it works changes size and container several times in one screen.
9. **The footer is one small line.** It carries the only links to Numbers, terms and what the board stores.

### 3. What the references do, in our terms

| Reference | What it does | What we take |
|---|---|---|
| Cognition | One strict column grid on every page, visibly; one typeface; long calm paragraphs; a small mark | One grid for the whole site; a mark |
| Resend | One line, one object, one filled button and one text link; the button repeated in the navigation | The action in the navigation; the hero already matches |
| Stripe's documentation | Dense reference made calm by a fixed left column of sections and short blocks | The shape for For agents and How it works |
| Apple, Muji (the owner's) | One idea per screen; the object does the talking; labels small and exact; nothing decorative | Already the front page's rule; apply it to the board's empty screen |

None of them fakes proof. We have no users to quote and no numbers to show, so the site should not pretend to; the product shown working is our proof.

### 5. For discussion

1. **The name.** One name on the site, the package and the tool, or keep three. This is the owner's alone; PF40 cannot draw a mark for a name that is not settled. My view: "omakase-box" is already the package and the outward identity; "Open bounty board" describes a page, not a product.
2. **An example on the empty board.** One bounty shown as it would look, plainly marked as an example, so an empty board still shows what the thing is. The owner cleared test records from the live board on 4 November; an example drawn by the page, never stored, is a different thing, but it is the owner's call.
3. **Dark first, or light first.** The site follows the visitor's setting today. Two of four references are dark. The owner's references (Muji, Apple) are light. Recommendation: keep following the setting; design light first.

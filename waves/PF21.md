# PF21: Your bounties, on the Owner page

Milestone: PF (the platform) · Station: packaging · Base: `master at dispatch` · Branch: `wave/PF21` · Timebox: 180 minutes; at expiry, report partial findings and stop
Owner decisions: D-2026-11-02-design-references-big-companies, D-2026-11-02-site-looks-need-approval, D-2026-11-03-cli-and-board-split
Horizon: short, with a mock-up for the owner's approval before code. Feature playbook; principles: experience-first, prove-it-works

**Brief.** Written by the CTO on 3 November 2026, from the owner in PF20: "the owner would not know of the bounties that have been created by the agents, which is so important… You want to be able to see all the bounties that your agents have created." The owner agreed it as the next wave.

## Outcome

On the Owner side of Your page, an owner sees every bounty their agents posted, not only those owing payment: each with its repository, reward, status and age, the newest first, each leading to its bounty page. A glance at the top says how many are open.

## Scope

- Write: `packages/box-platform/components/MeView.jsx`, `ui.jsx`, `src/account.ts` (`/api/me` gains the owner's bounties, by GitHub id), `tests/platform/`
- Do not touch: payments, charging, the CLI

## Build

1. **References, then a mock-up for the owner's approval before any code:** how big products list a person's own items with status (Stripe's and GitHub's lists, filter chips, empty states), read on their own pages and cited.
2. **The list:** "Your bounties" above Pay on merge: title, repository, reward, status (Open, Solutions waiting, Merged, Paid, Closed), age; Open and All as a quick switch; each row to `/b/<id>/`. Bounties are those whose issue GitHub says this person opened (the board's `poster_id`), so nothing is typed or claimed.
3. **The figure:** beside "You owe", how many bounties are open.
4. **Empty:** none posted yet says how to start (post one with the agent).
5. **Tests** that read the real page in each state, each new guard broken once; before-and-after full pages, both widths and themes; the owner's approval quoted; merge. The deploy needs its own word.

## References

- **Open / All switch:** Primer's segmented control, which says a segment that hides list items must sit beside one that shows the full list, with 2 to 5 choices and a text label (primer.style/product/components/segmented-control/guidelines). The count on each segment follows GitHub's own issue list ("Open 12 / Closed 4").
- **Filtering before the table:** Stripe Apps' filter-controls pattern says to filter the data before the table so its counts stay right, and that filter labels match the column names (docs.stripe.com/stripe-apps/patterns/filter-controls).
- **Empty states:** Primer's empty-state pattern calls for a title, a short why and one action (primer.style/product/ui-patterns/empty-states). The board's existing `Empty` already follows it.
- **Status:** ~~one coloured badge per row~~. The owner rejected the first mock-up ("looks basically like just a random app"). Second mock-up, from captured pages (bench `refs/`):
  - **Rows as a list, not a table:** GitHub's issue list (github.com/vercel/next.js/issues). The row has a state glyph, a 15px title that leads, and a quiet facts line (`repo #n · age`). Linear's views do the same: state icons rather than coloured pills, and the accent kept rare.
  - **Status words in plain text:** colour only where it means something (waiting, to pay, paid).
  - **Tabs with counts inside the list card:** GitHub's "Open 1,117 / Closed 24,573" header. Stripe's payment tabs add a "To pay" view, which replaces the separate To pay table.
  - **Figures as plain numbers on a hairline:** Mercury's and Stripe's overviews, with a large number and one line that leads to its rows.
  - **Pager:** Stripe's "1–20 of 57" with Previous and Next.
  - **Empty state:** Vercel Geist's icon, title, one line that adds information, and one action.
- **Mock-up 3** (bench `mock3/`). The owner said mock-up 2 was "in the right direction… could be refined":
  - **Grouping by status:** Linear's list views group rows by status, each group headed by a dot, a label and a count, so a row no longer repeats its status.
  - **Status dot and word:** Vercel's deployments list (its docs image `filter-status-light.png`).
  - **The solvers' GitHub pictures on the row:** GitHub's and Linear's avatar stacks.
  - **Figures as cards that lead to their rows:** Mercury's dashboard cards.
  - **Filled tabs and a quiet chevron on each row:** Linear's and Vercel's lists.
- **Mock-up 4** (bench `mock4/`). The owner, on mock-up 3: the tabs (Open, To pay, All) do not match the five categories, and all the groups in one long column read as linear and messy once each group is large. "Rethink… it's a common use case." The common use case is following items through stages, as with orders. Rebuilt on:
  - **The stages as the navigation:** four stages left to right (Waiting for a solution, Solutions waiting, Merged to pay, Paid), each with its count and money. They replace both the tabs and the two figures. Pipeline overviews (Stripe's and Mercury's figure cards, Linear's and GitHub Projects' status columns, which "always run from first to last status": linear.app/docs/board-layout).
  - **Closed without a merge sits apart:** it is not a stage on the way to paid.
  - **One stage at a time, with All as the second view:** NN/g on dashboards says one task per view, and to group with proximity and borders rather than colour alone (nngroup.com/articles/dashboards-preattentive). A large stage shows its first rows and then "Show more".
  - **Each row carries a small four-step bar:** Apple's order tracker (Baymard's example 16934). NN/g's status-tracker article says to put the latest status first (nngroup.com/articles/status-tracker-progress-update). Baymard found that users want the stages of delivery shown (baymard.com/ecommerce-design-examples/63-order-tracking-page).
  - **The list opens on the stage that needs the owner:** "Merged, to pay" when anything is owed.
- **Mock-up 5** (bench `mock5/`; `mock5.html` is clickable). The owner, on mock-up 4: the stage cards were not obviously clickable and looked plain. The per-row bar mixed up stage with solution count ("there are three solutions here but it says two solutions waiting"). Open-source kits browsed: shadcn/ui's dashboard and tasks examples (ui.shadcn.com/examples/dashboard and /tasks) and Tremor's KPI-card blocks (blocks.tremor.so/blocks/kpi-cards). The changes:
  - **The cards are a tab list that looks clickable:**
    - Each card has a lift on hover and says "View →".
    - The chosen card is ringed, says "Showing below ↓", and joins the table with a notch.
    - The cards show their step ("2/4") and have arrows between them.
    - A coloured edge on each card follows Tremor's KPI Card 9.
    - The heading says "Choose one to see its bounties".
  - **The count is unambiguous:** each card says "2 bounties", never "solutions".
  - **The table has fixed columns:** Bounty, Stage, Solutions, Merged, Reward and an action. Each fact has its own column, with the stage as an outlined pill with a dot. This follows shadcn/ui's data table (tinted header, status pill, "Page 1 of 2" pager). On phones each row becomes a labelled block.
  - **"All" and "Closed, none merged" are chips in the table bar.**
- **Mock-up 6** (bench `mock6/`; `mock6.html` is clickable). The owner, on mock-up 5: "Merged, to pay" reads like "Pay on merge" reversed. Pay on merge needs no action, so it should not sit in that stage. The look needed more polish, kept minimal (Muji, Apple). Screenshots taken in `refs/`: Radix Themes playground, HeroUI, Origin UI's stepper and tabs (coss.com/origin), Apple HIG segmented controls, and Muji. Changes:
  - **Stage 3 is "Payment due" and holds only what waits on the owner:** pay by link or by hand where pay on merge is off, or a failed automatic charge with its reason (the `charge-words.ts` states).
  - **A bounty paid by pay on merge goes from merged straight to Paid.** The Paid table's "Paid by" column says how: Pay on merge, One click (Stripe), or a payment you recorded.
  - **The stages are one large segmented control:**
    - Apple's HIG: "Use a segmented control to provide closely related choices that affect an object, state, or view."
    - It sits in a soft tray, like HeroUI's and Origin UI's controls.
    - Origin UI's numbered stepper line runs through it, so the order is part of the control.
    - The chosen stage is the raised white segment.
    - "Needs you" marks stage 3 when anything is due.
  - **The table's columns follow the stage:**
    - Stages 1 and 2: Solutions and Reward.
    - Stage 3: Merged, Payment and Reward, with the Pay button.
    - Stage 4: Merged, Paid by and Reward.
    - All: the Stage pill too.
    - The header is quieter, with no tint.
  - **Open question for the build:** a charge still in progress ("charging", seconds long) needs a place.
- **Mock-up 7** (bench `mock7/`, clickable `mock7.html`). The owner, on mock-up 6: the circles and the yellow, blue and green read as unpolished, "never seen at big companies". Phrases like "$85 offered" are too wordy, "Needs you" looks poor, the stage pills are plain colours, and the people icons are unimpressive. "Find the state-of-the-art libraries." Screenshots in `refs/`:
  - Catalyst, Tailwind Labs' application kit (catalyst-demo.tailwindui.com, its home and orders): overview figures under a hairline, and a borderless table of plain text.
  - Untitled UI React: outlined grey badges with a small dot.
  - Vercel Geist tabs: plain text, with an underline on the chosen tab.
- **The look, one ink and its greys:**
  - The stages are Catalyst-style figures: a hairline, a label, a large number and the amount in grey, with no words around them.
  - The chosen stage's hairline turns to ink, as Geist's tab underline does.
  - "Payment due" shows only a small accent dot.
  - On phones, the chosen stage gets a soft fill.
- **The table is borderless and in plain text, as Catalyst's is.** In All, the stage is a grey glyph that fills a quarter per stage and becomes a tick, with the stage name in grey.
- **People are a grey initial and the name.**
- **Colour appears only for the accent dot and a red failed-charge note.**
- **Mock-up 7, revised.** The owner liked the All view and asked why Payment due and Paid looked different. They did, because each stage had its own columns. Now every view has All's shape:
  - **Columns:** Bounty, Stage, Reward, and a Pay button where due.
  - **The Stage cell** has the glyph, the stage name, and one grey line for what matters there: the solution count, whose work was merged and how it was paid, or why payment is due. The earlier version is kept as `mock7-separate.html`.

- **After the mock-up:** the owner asked what Pay $60 did that the title did not. It only jumped to the same page, so it was removed. The title is the one way in, as in GitHub's and Stripe's lists.

### 2. Supply and demand

The documents already reach the right conclusion and I agree with it: **paid work is the scarce side, not solvers.** What I add is where the plan is thin.

- **The posting trigger is the weakest link, and nothing since K44 has moved it.** 0 of 3 agents handed off unprompted; 1 of 5 where it was defensible. Every later proof told the agent to post. The bet ("agents hand work off often") is still unmeasured.
- **The default produces bounties nobody takes.** With no answer the reward is $0, and our own advice tells an earning agent to decline $0. A solver who installs today and runs `discover` finds nothing, and after launch will find $0 work from one account. That is the first impression.
- **The door most likely to bring the first paid bounty is the one built last:** PF32, a maintainer listing an existing issue with a reward. It needs no agent to decide anything. Nothing in PF6 points at it.
- **After PF6 there is still no demand stage with an owner action in it.** Seeding and invitations are declined; what remains at $0 is registries, the kitchen's own real bounties, and waiting. That is a coherent choice; its honest reading is that launch is a measurement, not a growth event.
- **This kitchen has never handed off real work with its own tool unprompted.** It is the one posting agent we fully control and it has real tasks it cannot do (a Linux check before merge, a macOS check, the cloud browser route). Decision 8 of 4 November already allows it.

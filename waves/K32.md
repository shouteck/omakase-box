# K32: agents paying solvers, within the owner's mandate: the protocols compared and the pay stage designed

Milestone: K (kitchen) · Station: evidence (also packaging) · Base: master at dispatch · Branch: `wave/K32` · Timebox: 240 minutes; at expiry the cook stops and reports what it has

**Brief.** Written by the CTO on 18 October 2026 at the owner's direction: "agentic payment is solved… there are many agent tools that allow agents to make payments; we can learn from them… the end goal is to let agents make the decisions for everything in our tool, but by default we add guardrails such as having humans in the loop for important things." Recorded in PLAN.md as the autonomy principle. A research and design investigation: **it changes no product code and no test, creates no account, holds no key, and moves no money, real or test, outside a local stand-in.** Its product is a comparison, a design for the **pay** stage's autonomy, a prototype against a stand-in, and the outline of the build wave. Detailed for a cook without deep reasoning.

## Why

Today the **pay** stage ends with a message: after a merge, the tool tells the owner whom to pay, how much, and through the solver's `pay-to:` Stripe link (BT11b, `packages/box-bounty/pay.ts`); the owner pays by hand. The autonomy principle says every decision has a mode the owner chooses (the agent decides, ask me, off), defaults keep a human in the loop for money and anything public, and the owner loosens them by mandate. Payment is the decision furthest from that today. Since 2025, agent-payment protocols have appeared that address exactly this; a first look by the CTO (secondary sources, to be verified here):

- **x402** (Coinbase; now an x402 Foundation with Cloudflare): HTTP 402 revived; the agent pays in stablecoin inside the request flow, with no account or API key; spending limits live in wallets and SDKs, not the protocol.
- **AP2** (Google): signed **mandates**: an intent mandate (the user's conditions, limits, time limit), a cart mandate, a payment mandate signalling agent involvement; two modes, **human present** (the user approves) and **human not present** (the agent acts within the intent mandate).
- **ACP** (OpenAI and Stripe): checkout and order handoff, settled through Stripe; built for buying from merchants.
- **Card networks' agent tokens** (Visa, Mastercard): scoped card credentials for agents.

## Outcome

A recommendation for how a bounty's reward is paid to the merged solver by the posting agent within a mandate the owner set once, with "ask me each time" as the default; what the solver must provide to be paid; what it costs and risks; and how it fits the tool's rules. Or, if no option fits now, a plain statement of why and what would have to change.

## Constraints every option is held to

- **We never hold money.** Payment goes from the owner's own wallet, account or rail directly to the solver. No custody, no escrow run by us (PLAN.md, "Not in this plan").
- **The owner's authority is the mandate.** Nothing is paid outside limits the owner set: per bounty, per solver, per period, total, time limit. "Ask me each time" stays the default for money.
- **No agent sees a credential.** Keys and tokens are set up by the owner and used by the tool (as with `gh`'s sign-in); the agent never reads or types one.
- **The package stays one file with no dependencies** (BT12). A protocol needing a native library or a large SDK must say so, and the report weighs it.
- **Facts, never verdicts:** the tool states what it paid, to whom, under which mandate; it never judges whether a solution "deserves" payment beyond the owner's rule ("merged takes all").
- **Hard stops unchanged.** Payment touches money and an outside party: every real payment is the owner's alone until the owner decides otherwise (AGENTS.md).

## Method: five steps

**Step 1: the pay stage's questions (25 minutes).** From PLAN.md's roadmap and MEMORY-SPEC.md: what each actor asks at the pay moment (the owner: how much, to whom, within what limit, was it paid; the agent: may I pay, under which mandate; the solver: how do I get paid, will I; a later reader: what was paid and under which authority). These are the criteria.

**Step 2: the options, from primary sources (80 minutes).** For x402, AP2, ACP, the card networks' agent tokens, and today's Stripe link, one row each, with links and the date read: who pays and how (rail, currency); what the payee needs (an account, a wallet address, nothing); how limits and mandates are expressed and enforced (protocol, wallet, or ours to build); human-present versus not-present support; irreversibility and refunds; fees; who holds money in between (must be nobody but the parties); credentials (who creates them, where they live, can they be scoped to one purpose and limit); test modes; dependencies a single-file Node package would need; legal or regulatory notes the documentation itself raises (cite; do not give legal advice); maturity (specification version, adoption the documentation states). Add any other option found that fits better.

**Step 3: the design (50 minutes).** For the recommended option:
- **The mandate:** what the owner sets once, through `config` (the owner's surface stays `allow`, `deny`, `config`): rail, limits (per bounty, per solver, per period, total), time limit, and the mode for paying (the agent decides within the mandate; ask me each time, the default; off).
- **The flow:** merge recognised → the reward owed (F-reward, F-payee) → within the mandate and mode: pay, or hold and ask the owner exactly as merges are held (BT13) → the payment's proof recorded (F-paid, with the rail's reference) → the solver told on the issue, in the tool's marked comment, that payment was sent (a fact, no amount beyond what the issue already states).
- **What the solver provides** in place of, or beside, today's `pay-to:` link, and how the tool reads it safely (strangers' text: BT11b's rules).
- **Failure and safety:** duplicate payments (idempotency keys), a payment failing midway, a forged payee line, a mandate exhausted, the owner revoking it.
- **Where it lives** in the code (the pay stage's modules) and what MEMORY-SPEC.md gains.

**Step 4: the prototype against a stand-in (60 minutes).** In `kitchen/payments/`, scratch code: a local stand-in of the chosen rail's payment endpoint, answering in the shapes its specification documents (quote them in comments); a scripted run: a merged bounty with a $5 reward under a $20 mandate is paid once; a second payment for the same bounty is refused as a duplicate; a $25 reward exceeds the mandate and is held for the owner; a revoked mandate refuses; the proof is recorded. No real network, wallet or money.

**Step 5: the report and the build outline (25 minutes).** The build outline must also cover the manual record folded in from BT36 (`waves/BT36.md`): the owner, or their agent on the owner's word, records a payment made by hand (BT36 proposed `box bounty config set paid <id>`, with `no` to withdraw; amount copied from the bound reward, never typed; only for a merged bounty that owes money; shown as owed or paid; a `paid` event in the shared memory, BT32), as the fallback beside automatic payment. The recommendation, its cost and risks, the owner's one-time setup counted in steps, what changes in PLAN.md ("Not in this plan" keeps holding anyone's money), and the outline of the build wave or waves.

Findings (none blocks this research wave; each is for the CTO's brief of BT37/BT38 or the owner's page):

1. **The cross-machine duplicate guarantee holds only for one payer wallet.** ERC-3009: "Nonces are randomly generated 32-byte data unique to the authorizer's address" (E1, `authorizationState(authorizer, nonce)`). The derived nonce (`kitchen/payments/pay-stage.mjs:105`) is unique per repository and bounty, but the chain lock is per `from`: if the owner changes `pay-from`, or two machines are configured with different wallets, the same bounty gets a fresh nonce space and the token contract will pay again; only the ledger rule stops it. The report's table (`kitchen/reviews/K32-agent-payments.md:165`) and line 81 omit this, and do not say that a derived nonce departs from the EIP's description of nonces as random (not a requirement: the contract checks only use). Build wave: record `payer` in the shared `paid` event, make rule 1 read the shared chain (BT32) before paying, and check `authorizationState` against every payer the repository's mandate has used; also fix the canonical repository string (renames, case).
2. **The public payment comment exposes the owner's wallet.** The design posts "transaction `<hash>` on Base" on the issue (`K32-agent-payments.md:154`). A transaction hash publicly ties the owner's GitHub account to their wallet's whole balance and history; BT36 held that "payment is private between owner and solver; nothing is posted" (`waves/BT36.md:27`). The owner's page risks (line 17) do not say it. Recommend the comment omit the hash (the solver sees it in their wallet), or the owner's page states it.

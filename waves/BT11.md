# BT11: a stated reward, and the solver's own payment link

Milestone: BT · Station: packaging (also evidence) · Base: master at the time this cooks, after BT10 is merged · Branch: `wave/BT11` · Timebox: 150 minutes; at expiry, report partial findings and stop

> **Changed by the owner's decisions of 12 October 2026. Where the text below differs, this wins; the head chef folds it in when revising the brief at dispatch.**
> - **The owner sets the reward, and only the owner (13 October 2026).** One amount, carried by every bounty. An agent pricing bounties itself was considered and left out as too early. See "The reward the owner states" below.
> - **There is no award to make.** The solver whose solution is merged is the one to pay, all of it. The `reward` command is not built; the sections below are rewritten accordingly.
> - **Nothing is asked of the agent in words:** no reason, no choice among solvers, no second solver.
> - **The terms in the issue** say that one solution is merged and earns the reward and that other solutions earn nothing, including where parts are used (BT8, BT10).

**Brief, written ahead.** By the CTO on 11 October 2026, while BT6's second run was cooking. The idea is the owner's and is settled; it is deliberately the simplest thing that could work. Command names, field names and file paths follow the design discussion; the head chef checks them against the Results of BT6a to BT10 before dispatching, corrects names only, and says in the pre-cook card what changed.

## The idea, as the owner put it

No bounty site in the middle. A bounty can state a reward. The issue tells the solver to put their own payment link in their pull request, Stripe links only to start. If the agent or owner decides a solver should be paid, the link is right there. **The tool never pays, never opens the link, and never holds or moves anyone's money.** It carries two facts (the amount the owner's rule states, and where the solver asked for it to be sent) and records a third (that the agent awarded that solver).

## Who decides, and who pays

**The merge decides. The owner pays, by opening the solver's link** (owner, 11 and 12 October 2026). The agent's judgement is which solution it merges; being merged is what makes a solution the worthy one, and no owner approves that. When the tool closes a bounty with a merged solution, it gives the agent the message for the owner: whom to pay, the amount and the link. The agent passes it on and **assumes the owner will faithfully pay**. Nobody is asked to confirm, and the tool does not track whether the payment happened: it cannot know, and until agent wallets exist there is nothing it could do about it. What the owner then does, and what the agent does about a solver it did not award, is theirs; the tool has no "paid", no "declined" and no opinion. Every solution stays on GitHub exactly as its solver left it.

An agent paying by itself is left out, in the backlog, until agent wallets work without a person: on that date Stripe's wallet for agents still needed the owner's approval for every purchase, and could only be tried with real money. Nothing in this wave builds toward it, and the tool's outputs name the owner as the one who pays.

## Outcome

1. An owner's standing rule can state a reward above $0, and every bounty posted under it says so.
2. The issue asks a solver who wants paying for one fixed line in their pull request.
3. Next to each solution the agent sees, as a fact, whether a recognised payment link was given.
4. When a solution is merged and the tool closes the bounty, it gives the agent the message to pass to the owner: whom to pay, how much, and where. The solver to pay is the one whose solution was merged; no command awards anyone.

**The reward the owner states (owner, 13 October 2026: the owner only, kept simple)**

- The owner's settings (BT8) state one reward, in US dollars as a whole number, at either level. Every bounty carries it, in the listing and in the readable block. 0 is valid and means what it means today.
- The agent is asked nothing about money. No command lets an agent choose, change or raise an amount, for one bounty or for all.
- A bounty keeps the amount it was posted with. Changing the setting changes later bounties only; the tool says so when the setting changes while bounties are open.
- **A limit on what is promised at once (owner, 13 October 2026).** With a reward above 0 the owner also states the most that may be promised across open bounties; the tool refuses to accept a reward above 0 without it, and says why. A post that would pass it is a named refusal that says both numbers and changes nothing. The limit counts open bounties only: when a bounty closes, merged or not, its amount no longer counts. The tool keeps no record of what was paid and has no command for it. `status` and the summary show the limit, what is promised by open bounties and what is left. Without this, a reward of $20 and fifty bounties is $1,000 promised in the owner's name with nobody asked.
- The first time the owner states any amount above 0, the tool says once, in plain words, what that is: a promise, made in the owner's name, that the owner pays by hand.
- The tool states no preference about amounts and suggests none.
- Left out on purpose, for a later wave if real use asks for it: an agent setting the price of each bounty within a budget, and raising a posted reward. The owner judged it too early and likely to confuse.

**What the issue tells the solver**

- When the reward is above $0, the "how to answer" part of the listing adds, in plain words: the reward; that a solver who wants to be paid adds one line to their pull request description, `pay-to:` followed by their Stripe payment link, with a worked example; and that payment is the poster's decision and posting a solution does not guarantee it. The readable block says the same in fields a program can use, and the format's version changes with a note on the public page.
- At $0 none of this appears. The listing says "Reward: $0" and nothing about payment links, exactly as before this wave.
- A test pins the wording: no sentence in it promises payment, timing or selection.

**Reading the solver's line**

- The line is text written by a stranger and is treated as that. The tool reads only the pull request description, takes only the first line that starts with `pay-to:`, and accepts what follows only if it is exactly one address of the recognised kind: `https`, the host Stripe uses for its payment links, and a path of the shape those links have, with nothing after it. The worker confirms the current shapes from Stripe's own documentation and lists them in the doc page; anything not on that list is not recognised.
- What the agent sees next to each solution is one of four facts and nothing else from that line: a recognised payment link was given; a test-mode payment link was given (no real money moves through it); a `pay-to:` line was given that is not a recognised payment link; none was given. Unrecognised text is never echoed.
- **The tool never opens, fetches or follows the link,** at any step, in any mode. A test proves no network call is made with it. So the tool cannot know what amount the link asks for, and says so: when it shows a link it states the amount the owner's rule gives and that whoever pays must see that same amount at checkout.
- A link is shown in full only by the command that marks a solver as to be paid, and there it is on its own line, labelled as supplied by the solver.

**Telling the owner whom to pay**

- No command awards anyone. When the tool closes a bounty because a solution was merged (BT7b), and the reward is above $0, the closing output carries the message for the owner, whichever command noticed the merge.
- The message is addressed to the agent, ready to relay as it stands:
  - the bounty, by title and identifier, with the address of its issue;
  - the solver, as their GitHub account, and **the address of their pull request**, which is the one thing worth opening before paying: it shows what the solver did, that they are its author, and the `pay-to:` line itself. Both addresses are built by the tool from the repository and the numbers it recorded, never copied from text a stranger wrote;
  - the commit in which the solution was merged;
  - the amount, marked as coming from the owner's setting;
  - the solver's link, on its own line, marked as supplied by the solver, with one sentence to check that the checkout shows that amount and to look at the account name shown there;
  - who last edited the pull request description the link was read from, when that was not the solver.
- If the merged solution gave no recognised link, the message says so as a fact and gives the pull request's address; nothing else changes and nobody is asked anything.
- At $0, or when a bounty is closed with nothing merged, there is no message.
- The message is a statement, not a question: "this solution was merged; pay here". It does not ask for approval, a reply or a confirmation.
- **The agent then treats the matter as settled.** The tool keeps no waiting state for the payment, sends no reminder, and has no command to record a payment or a refusal.
- **The owner is told twice, in the same session, and that is all.** Once when the bounty closes, in the conversation. Once more at the end of the agent's work, where an owner who was away will look first: the closing output tells the agent to repeat the message in its closing summary, and on hosts where the hook has a slot for the moment an agent is about to finish (BT9), the hook shows it there, so the repeat does not depend on the agent remembering. Nothing is carried to a later session: the agent assumes the owner will faithfully pay, so there is nothing to chase.
- `status` and the owner's `summary` still list every closed bounty with whom to pay, as a record the owner can read whenever they wish. They are not reminders.
- `status` shows, per bounty: the reward, for each solution whether a link was given, and for a closed bounty which solution was merged.
- Under standing rules, awarding needs no extra permission: it moves no money. Nothing in this wave lets the tool or the agent complete a payment.
- When the bounty is closed, the tool's comment on the issue states only what is in the record: which solutions were used and which solvers the poster's agent awarded. It does not say that anyone was or will be paid, and it says nothing about solutions that were neither used nor awarded. Closing changes nothing in any solver's pull request beyond what closing the bounty branch does to all of them.
- The tool sends nothing by itself: no email, no message, no comment addressed to the owner.

**Everything else**

- **Cases that must fail, each a named refusal that changes nothing:** an amount in the rules that is not a whole number of dollars at or above 0; a reward field anywhere in the project; `reward` for a solution that does not exist; `reward` on a solution whose line is not a recognised link.
- **A stranger's words are not the tool's words,** as since BT6: hostile text in the `pay-to:` line (a second address, text after the address, another scheme, a lookalike host, control characters, a very long line, a line placed in a comment or a file instead of the description) never reaches output except as "not a recognised payment link", and never reaches the hook.
- The three hard stops are unchanged. A never-ship path, a private repository and the owner's rules are not affected by a reward.
- Bounties, records and rules written before this wave load and read as $0.
- The description shown to agents says, briefly, that a bounty may carry a reward if the owner's rules state one. The doc page gains one section, including in plain words what the tool does not do: pay, know whether a payment was made, hold money, or protect a solver from not being paid.
- Works on this Windows host from Git Bash and PowerShell, and on the off-machine runner.

## The real use: no real money

The owner's standing instruction for testing: prove the flow, spend nothing, the kitchen is the solver.

1. In a trial repository of the kitchen's own, with a trial settings folder: an owner's rule states a reward, a bounty goes out, two solutions come back (one with a recognised link, one with hostile text on the line), a collecting session marks one to be paid and records it as paid. All local or against stand-ins.
2. **One real bounty on `shouteck/omakase-box`,** from the main checkout with the merged tool. Its title and first line say it is a test bounty by the repository's owner, to be solved by the owner, and that no real money will be paid; real people can read it and must not be misled. The stated reward is $1. **The owner sees the exact issue text and says "go" before anything is posted.**
3. The kitchen's blind solver opens the pull request with a `pay-to:` line carrying a **test-mode** Stripe payment link. **The owner made one for this, in test mode, on 11 October 2026, for $1:** `https://buy.stripe.com/test_28E7sLbHrbu70eC5C5afS00`. Use exactly that. It moves no real money and is safe to appear in a public pull request. No agent creates a Stripe account, signs in to one or makes a link. If the link has stopped working by the time this cooks, tell the owner; do not substitute another.
4. A collecting session, new and told nothing, sees the fact "a test-mode payment link was given", merges that solution, and passes on the message the tool gives when it closes the bounty. That is the end of the tool's part and of the agent's. **No agent opens the link, enters any payment detail or completes any checkout,** test mode or not. The owner has said they will open the test link and pay with one of Stripe's published test cards; the kitchen notes in the Result whether the owner said they did, as the owner's word, and the tool records nothing about it.
5. Closed with the tool's own commands. Anything left on GitHub is listed in the Result by exact name.

What this proves: every step the tool takes around a reward, on real GitHub. What it does not prove: that a real stranger will supply a link, that a real payment completes, or anything about an agent paying by itself. The Result says exactly that.

Nothing in this wave is posted or closed overnight.

## Review lenses

- [x] **Security:** the `pay-to:` line is the first place a stranger's text sits next to money. The reviewer tries to get a link of its choosing shown as recognised (lookalike hosts, mixed scripts, user-info in the address, redirects written into the path, a second line, encoded characters), to get any text from the line into `status`, the hook or the issue comment, to make the tool fetch the address, to get an amount from anywhere but the owner's rule into a listing or an output, and to get a solver recorded as awarded without the `reward` command, and to change who or what the message to the owner names (a swapped link, an edited description, a second `pay-to:` line). It confirms no payment detail, of anyone, is read, stored or printed.
- [x] **User experience:** three readers, each fresh. A stranger with only the issue says what the reward is, exactly what line to add and where, and what it has and has not been promised. An agent with two solutions waiting and only the tool's output says which can be paid through a link and which cannot, awards one, and says what happens next and who does it. An owner, played by the reviewer with only the message the agent passes on, says whom they are to pay, how much, why their agent chose them, how they know the address came from the solver, and what nobody has checked.
- [ ] **Measurement fairness:** not applicable.

## Notes for the worker

- Feature playbook. This wave is small on purpose. If it grows a wallet, a verifier, an escrow, a second payment provider, it has gone wrong. The one limit on what is promised at once is the owner's decision of 13 October 2026 and is in; anything more about money is not.
- The parser for the line is the security boundary. Write it as an allow-list of exact shapes, with the tests first, and reject by default.
- Every output about money is a fact with its source: "from your rule", "supplied by the solver", "awarded by your agent". BT3a's banned-words test covers them all.
- Do not make marking a solver depend on the solution having been used. The owner settled that these are independent.
- Out of scope, each a later decision of the owner's: an agent paying by itself with a wallet or a capped card (in the backlog until agent wallets work without a person); any payment provider other than Stripe links; checking that a payment happened; a ceiling or budget (nothing in this wave can spend); disputes; any bounty site.
- Everything the wave creates in the temp folder or on the remote is named for the wave or the bounty and removed at close.

# K46: how an agent pays a solver with no human: the route chosen, or the blocker named

Milestone: K (kitchen) · Station: evidence · Base: `master at dispatch` · Branch: `wave/K46` · Timebox: 300 minutes; at expiry, report partial findings and stop
Owner decisions: D-2026-11-01-agent-only-direction, D-2026-10-21-no-platform-money
Horizon: long: start with `/plan` and keep the plan in this file

**Brief.** Written by the CTO on 1 November 2026. Research and decision only: **nothing goes into the product, no real money moves, no account is created and no lawyer is engaged.** It does not block PF9, K41, PF6 or the launch, which ship the guard-rail version.

## Why

The product's direction is agent-only: posting, solving, choosing and paying with no person involved, as an option the owner turns on, with today's guard rails still the default. Every step but payment already works that way. Today a person pays.

Earlier work did not settle this. K32 designed a pay stage and recommended by-hand first, then x402 with the owner's mandate. K42 and K42b studied a platform moving money through Stripe, and D-2026-10-21-no-platform-money ruled that out. K42b also stopped stablecoins at a legal question. K46 starts from those and asks only what they left open.

## The question

How can the posting agent pay the solver directly, within a limit the owner set, with the platform never charging, holding or moving funds?

## Outcome

A report the owner can decide from, in `docs/box-v4/AGENT-PAYMENT.md`: one recommended route, or a plain statement that none is ready and what would have to change. Its first page is the owner's: what the route is, what it costs, what the owner would set up once, what could go wrong, and the one decision.

## What it does

1. **Starts from what we have.** Read `kitchen/reviews/K32-agent-payments.md`, `docs/box-v4/PLATFORM-MONEY-SG.md` and `docs/box-v4/PLATFORM.md`. Restate what still holds and what has changed since. Do not redo their work.
2. **Compares the routes, read on the day, each with sources:**
   - stablecoin wallet to wallet, direct or through x402;
   - an agent's virtual card (AgentCard and others) paying a link the solver already lists;
   - agent payment toolkits from existing payment companies;
   - anything newer the search finds.
3. **Holds every route to the same table:**
   - **No person:** can an agent pay, and can a solver agent receive, with no human step after the owner's one-time setup? Name each identity check and who must pass it.
   - **The owner's limit:** is it enforced outside the agent, so an agent cannot talk its way past it?
   - **The platform:** does our code or board hold, move or route funds at any point?
   - **Reach, cost and mistakes:** which countries, what fees on a $50 bounty, what happens on a wrong or duplicate payment, and what happens when a stranger's text tries to redirect a payment.
4. **Draws the design with the least legal exposure.** The Singapore Payment Services Act covers "arranging" the transmission of digital payment tokens (K42b section 5). Compare where the line could fall: the board showing an address the solver listed; the tool printing it; the tool handing it to the agent's own wallet tool; the tool signing or sending. **The report does not give a legal conclusion.** It writes the exact questions for a Singapore lawyer, so the owner can decide whether to ask.
5. **Proves the leading route on the bench, at $0,** if it can run with no account: two agents' scripts on a local test chain or the protocol's own local stand-in. Posting agent pays solver within a limit, a payment over the limit is refused, and a duplicate does not pay twice. Scratch code outside the product; removed at close. If the route cannot be exercised without an account or real money, the report says so.
6. **Sketches the option, not the build:** where the owner's switch would live (`config`, never a new command), how "ask me each time" still holds, what `paid` records, and what the first build wave would contain. Human payment stays the default.

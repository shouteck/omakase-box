## The problem, stated precisely

Not yet formulated; that is the next stage. The survey leaves three statements that can each be shown wrong:

1. *Nothing in use chooses the source for one task from a failing example and sends it out with that example as the check.* Wrong if such a thing exists. Pieces do: partial returnable repository views, subset-mounting sandboxes, deny lists.
2. *A Box costs the owner more to prepare than the incumbent (an issue and the whole repository to a trusted agent), and gives the solver less.* Wrong if making one takes a step and the solver does as well.
3. *There are solvers an owner wants to use and will not give the repository to.* Unknown, and not answerable from public sources.

## Where the field is

The problem goes by crowdsourced software development, bounties, microtask programming, onboarding, offshoring extra costs, and, since 2025, multi-agent delegation and least privilege for agents. Handing bounded work to outsiders stayed small in each human form; two case studies put the weight on the owner's costs, and none measures whether disclosure deterred owners. Delegation to agents on the whole repository did take hold. Sub-agents get a brief and the whole working directory; vendors are adding deny-style path controls. ## Current best answer

After the sceptic. The evidence *is consistent with* a Box standing or falling on the owner's cost to make one. It *suggests* that the only new part of a Box is the rule that chooses the source. It does not separate the candidate directions (least-privilege hand-off to a less-trusted solver; the unit an orchestrator passes inside one boundary), does not show that anyone wants either, and does not rule out stopping. The three measurements that would decide are the lab's to make: the size of Line A's region, a solver on a Box against the same solver on the repository, and the owner's cost of the example.

# Validate delegation value

CEO direction, 5 October 2026: portable Boxes and owner-chosen contributors are an MVP hypothesis. Demonstrate reduced delegation effort and repeat use before treating the positioning as a competitive advantage. This document defines future work; it does not approve execution or a customer study.

## Initial customer and job

Start with engineering teams that regularly engage contractors for bounded changes in an existing repository. The owner has technical authority to review and integrate work. PMs and founders can initiate requests through the same preparation path; broad consumer adoption is a later hypothesis.

The job is: prepare a change, give the chosen contributor enough context and a usable environment, receive a contribution, and establish that it fits the original system. Test portable selected-source Boxes first on the supported non-sensitive fixtures. An invited contractor with owner-repository access remains a legitimate execution mode, but is not a portable-package success or a confidentiality guarantee.

## Competitive evidence and limits

Public materials checked on 5 October 2026 show substantial overlap:

- GitStart's [Ticket Studio](https://gitstart.com/studio) describes codebase-grounded specifications for engineers and coding agents. Its [repository guide](https://gitstart.com/getting-started/2-repo-config/) describes sliced repositories and build validation. Its [service documentation](https://gitstart.com/docs) describes dedicated delivery teams. These are published capabilities and positioning, not an independent performance benchmark. Do not infer that every GitStart capability requires its managed workforce.
- [Algora's own repository](https://github.com/algora-io/algora) describes contractor management, outcome-based payments and internal bounty programmes for open and closed source teams.
- [GitHub Copilot documentation](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/how-tos/use-copilot-agents/cloud-agent/use-cloud-agent-on-github) describes task delegation and returned pull requests. [Coder](https://coder.com/solutions/workspaces) describes governed workspaces for developers and agents.

Self-service, chosen executors, code selection and human/agent support alone do not establish novelty or defensibility. Actual comparative claims need observed comparable runs. Do not procure competitor access or send project data to a vendor without applicable authority.

## Define the comparison in D1

D1.1 specifies an ordinary issue-plus-contributor baseline, two supported requests and an unsupported case before looking at final results. Record the same source baseline, business outcome, acceptance obligations, contributor familiarity, tool/model support, dependency cache, runtime and owner access for both routes. The baseline may legitimately grant repository access; the Box route must preserve its declared package-only restriction. These access differences are part of what is being tested, not something to hide.

Predeclare success and failure classifications, observation fields and the practical improvement being tested. No arbitrary percentage saving is already promised. Do not execute excluded baseline code on the host, recruit participants or consume paid resources merely to fill a comparison. If a comparable baseline is not yet authorised or possible, state the missing evidence and propose the smallest suitable pilot.

## Collect evidence through D2-D3

For every attempt, record:

| Measure | What counts |
|---|---|
| Owner preparation | Scoping, agreement clarification, context selection, package/access setup and preparation corrections |
| Recipient onboarding | Time to a usable baseline, setup failures, context search and owner questions |
| Implementation | Contributor effort on the actual change; keep separate from onboarding |
| Owner acceptance | Independent verification, review, integration, rejection and rework |
| Elapsed delivery | Waiting and handoffs, separately from active human effort and agent/compute usage |
| Quality and transfer | Accepted outcome, defects, required omissions, transferred source/dependencies and access granted |

Record common repository-onboarding costs separately from per-Box effort; do not omit them. Use observed durations, interventions and receipts. Missing observations remain missing, not zero. Include failures, abandoned attempts and unsupported requests in the denominator. Do not claim reduced effort by moving hidden work to the owner, weakening acceptance or supplying the full owner source to the portable recipient.

Compare equivalent quality and obligations before interpreting time differences. Reusing a task, recipient or fixture can produce learning effects; counterbalance task order where practical or disclose the resulting limitation. A small synthetic agent comparison establishes neither human usability nor market demand. Use descriptive results and limitations rather than unsupported statistical generalisation.

## D4 separates capability from customer value

D4.1 verifies actual package-only contributions and negative cases. D4.2 reconciles all effort observations and any authorised baseline, then reports four separate conclusions: software capability, measured delegation effort, actual repeat use and commercial commitment. An absent customer study remains explicitly unverified and does not fabricate a software failure or success in the market.

Prepare a customer-pilot protocol and blank recording instruments as part of the research deliverable. Any real pilot has a separate approved brief for participants, data access, messages, resources and payment terms. Invite-only proprietary access does not waive that scope. Evidence distinctions:

- Interview interest: a participant says the workflow sounds useful.
- Repeat use: the team chooses a Box for a subsequent eligible real task; record offered opportunities and reasons for declining as well as returning teams.
- Commercial intent: a buyer agrees to specific pricing/terms; identify whether it is hypothetical, conditional or a signed commitment.
- Payment: an actual authorised paid use is recorded. Never describe interest or a trial as revenue.

The core pilot question is whether the team chooses Boxes for its next delegation and why. Pilot thresholds and price experiments are frozen in the future pilot brief; the kitchen does not invent a passing threshold afterward.

## Next decision

Recommend one of: extend the supported task class where capability and effort results justify it; simplify packaging where preparation outweighs savings; conduct the smallest authorised customer pilot where usefulness is unknown; or reframe/stop where the measured job is served better by ordinary delegation. Paid adoption or repeat use is not assumed from a successful demo. A marketplace, custom IDE and universal extraction are not responses to missing differentiation evidence.

Station responsibilities: product owns buyer/job and understandable workflow; platform owns usable packages and preparation/setup instrumentation; assurance owns fair comparisons, independent acceptance, denominators and claim limits.
